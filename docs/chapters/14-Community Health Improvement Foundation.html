15  Community Health Improvement Foundation

In the MAPP 2.0 framework, creating a Community Health Improvement Plan is essential because it translates data and assessments into a roadmap for collective action. It allows a community to move beyond simply identifying problems to aligning resources across sectors to tackle root causes of health inequities. The foundation of an effective CHIP rests on broad community ownership, a strategic focus on upstream root causes, and a commitment to actionable accountability through measurable goals and continuous evaluation.

15.1 2022–2024 CHIP Final Review

In the 2022–2024 Orange County CHIP, two overarching priority areas were selected from the 2019–2024 New York State DOH Prevention Agenda: Preventing Chronic Disease and Promoting Well-Being and Preventing Mental Health and Substance Use Disorders.

15.1.1 Focus Areas

Within the priority area of Preventing Chronic Disease, the following focus areas and goals were chosen (numbers corresponding to the 2019–2024 New York State Prevention Agenda):

Focus Area 1: Healthy Eating and Food Security

  • Goal 1.1: Increase access to healthy and affordable foods and beverages
  • Goal 1.3: Increase food security

Focus Area 4: Preventative Care and Management

  • Goal 4.1: Increase cancer screening rates for breast, cervical, and colorectal cancer

Within the priority area of Promoting Well-Being and Preventing Mental Health and Substance Use Disorders, the following focus areas and goals were chosen (numbers corresponding to the 2019–2024 New York State Prevention Agenda):

Focus Area 2: Mental and Substance Use Disorders Prevention

  • Goal 2.2: Prevent Opioid and other Substance Misuse and Deaths

15.2 2026–2030 CHIP Roadmap

To drive the Community Health Improvement Plan (CHIP) process, OCDOH established a Steering Committee comprising over 20 diverse stakeholders, including area hospitals, community-based organizations, business owners, schools, Federally Qualified Health Centers, and the broader community. The Steering Committee is tasked with reviewing assessments to collectively select priority areas and evidence-based strategies; drafting goals and objectives; and establishing partner assignments, evaluation measures, and outcome metrics.

In January, the Steering Committee conducted a comprehensive review of the Orange County Community Health Assessment, the Mid-Hudson Regional Community Health Assessment, and qualitative insights from the OC Health Summit, surveys, and focus groups. To synthesize this vast dataset, the committee analyzed trend data, regional comparisons, and health disparities, prioritizing issues based on the availability of evidence-based interventions and measurable outcome data.

In February, the Steering Committee finalized the selection of three priority areas: Food Security, Cancer Screening, and Mental Health. Each priority area will be supported by an OCDOH-led workgroup that leverages existing partnerships to actively recruit residents and community partners. The Steering Committee will meet monthly to develop the final 2026–2030 CHIP for submission to the NYSDOH by June 30, 2026.

Following submission, the Committee will shift to quarterly meetings to monitor progress, review current data, identify the need for resources, and implement the MAPP 2.0 Action Cycle. This ongoing evaluation ensures each strategy remains responsive to community needs and is effectively executed through 2030.
