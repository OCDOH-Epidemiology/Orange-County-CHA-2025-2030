10  Health Behaviors

Health behaviors include the actions and habits that influence an individual’s health status, longevity, and risk of preventable disease and injury. This section summarizes key measures across two domains—Substance Use and Injury Prevention & Safety—using the tables and figures listed below.

10.1 Substance Use

Substance use patterns reflect both individual behavior and broader community conditions that shape risk exposure, access to treatment, and the likelihood of adverse outcomes. In Orange County, substance-use–related indicators show improvement across several acute-care burden measures, while also demonstrating persistent concentration of overdose mortality by age and sex and ongoing youth access patterns driven largely by social sourcing.

10.1.1 Hospitalizations

Substance use hospitalizations (ED visits) and neonatal abstinence show a decline over the years. ED visit rates for diagnoses related to any drug use declined from 194.5 per 100,000 (2019) to 178.0 (2022), after a higher value in 2020 Figure 10.1. Opioid-related ED visit rates also declined from 175.5 (2019) to 114.1 (2022) Figure 10.2. Neonatal abstinence syndrome rates decreased from 9.2 per 1,000 births (2019) to 6.7 (2022) Figure 10.3.

10.1.2 Controlled Substance Prescribing Patterns

Controlled substance prescribing indicators trend downward overall, with one prescribing-risk metric increasing modestly. The opioid prescribing rate declined from 292.5 per 1,000 person-years (2021) to 261.8 (2024) Figure 10.4, and benzodiazepine prescribing decreased from 245.1 (2021) to 221.3 (2024) Figure 10.6. Co-prescribing overlap (opioid + benzodiazepine) declined from 9.1% (2021) to 8.0% (2024) (Figure 127). In contrast, the share of opioid-naïve patients receiving a 7+ day supply increased from 14.9% (2021) to 15.8% (2024) Figure 10.5. Treatment-related prescribing measures show buprenorphine prescribing declining from 610.5 (2021) to 499.6 (2024) Figure 10.7, while the percentage receiving continuous buprenorphine increased from 71.3% (2021) to 74.2% (2024) Figure 10.8.

10.1.3 Youth Substance Use and Perception

Youth perception and use patterns show clear grade gradients for several substances, while marijuana stands out for lower perceived risk and measurable current use. Perceived “moderate/great risk” increases by grade for alcohol and cigarettes (e.g., cigarettes rise from 76.1% in grade 8 to 87.6% in grade 12), while marijuana shows lower perceived risk overall and declines by grade (65.0% grade 8 to 54.7% grade 12) Figure 10.9. Lifetime use also rises by grade across multiple substances; for example, lifetime alcohol use increases from 12.4% (grade 8) to 46.8% (grade 12), and lifetime marijuana use from 7.0% to 17.3% Table 10.9. Past-30-day use is lower in absolute terms but identifies ongoing behaviors, including energy drink use (31.1% total), alcohol (13.0%), marijuana (5.4%), and nicotine vaping (5.4%) Table 10.10. Among students reporting marijuana use, the most frequently reported access was getting it from someone under age 21 (49.2%), followed by someone age 21+ (21.5%) and a party (21.5%), with additional access from home without permission (18.0%) or with permission (10.6%) Table 10.11.

10.1.4 Overdose Fatalities

Overdose fatalities declined from 2021 to 2024 but remain highly concentrated by age and sex. The opioid-related overdose death rate decreased from 36.7 per 100,000 (2021) to 19.3 (2024) Figure 10.10, with corresponding annual counts documented in the overdose fatality table Table 10.13. Age-stratified rates show that adults ages 35–44 experienced the highest rates, reaching 110.6 per 100,000 in 2024 Figure 10.11, Table 10.13. Sex disparities persist across all years shown, with male overdose death rates substantially higher than female rates (e.g., 53.6 vs. 20.1 per 100,000 in 2021 and 29.7 vs. 9.0 in 2024) Table 10.13.

While key indicators are summarized here, the complete Annual Report on Drug-Related Mortality6 can be accessed via the OCDOH website.

10.1.5 Alcohol Use

Alcohol indicators show improvement in some behaviors alongside continued harm signals. Adult binge drinking declined from 15.7% (2016) to 13.8% (2021) Figure 10.12. In contrast, alcohol-involved motor vehicle mortality was 38.7 per 100,000 in 2021, higher than the state average of 25.4 per 100,000 Figure 10.13. Youth alcohol access was largely social/family-mediated: the most common reported source was from home with parental permission (45.7%), followed by at a party (39.7%) and from someone under 21 (27.0%) Table 10.16.

10.1.6 Smoking

Smoking indicators were comparatively stable across the years available. Adult current smoking was 13.2% (2016), 11.7% (2018), and 12.0% (2021) Figure 10.14.

10.2 Injury Prevention & Safety

Injury prevention and safety behaviors reflect the decisions and environmental conditions that influence injury risk, avoidable hospitalization, and preventable mortality. Indicators in Orange County show differences by race/ethnicity, age, sex, and geography, with additional burden from falls among older adults and measurable adolescent vulnerability for self-inflicted injury hospitalizations.

10.2.1 Unintentional Injury

Unintentional hospitalization and mortality show clear subgroup and geographic differences. Unintentional injury hospitalization rates (2020–2022 average) were 70.1 per 10,000 in Orange County, with variation by race/ethnicity (e.g., Non-Hispanic White: 68.4; Hispanic: 42.0; Non-Hispanic Asian: 26.6) Figure 10.15. Unintentional injury mortality increases markedly with age; for example, the 85+ age group reached 455.8 per 100,000 in 2022 Figure 10.17, Table 10.16. Across the four city ZIP codes shown, the 2020–2023 average ranged from 68.2 (ZIP 10940) to 159.5 (ZIP 12550) Figure 10.18, Table 10.16. Age-adjusted injury mortality rates, stratified by sex, show higher mortality among males than females across all years shown Figure 10.19.

10.2.2 Falls

Falls among adults age 65+ represent a major injury driver, with subgroup differences present. Falls hospitalization rates among adults age 65+ (2020–2022 average) were 212.8 per 10,000 in Orange County, with higher rates among Non-Hispanic White older adults (222.8) compared with other groups shown Figure 10.20.

10.2.3 Self-harm

Self-harm and suicide indicators show adolescent vulnerability alongside broader injury-related mortality burden. Self-inflicted injury hospitalization rates declined from 5.7 per 10,000 (2018) to 4.5 (2021) Figure 10.21. Among ages 15–19, self-inflicted injury hospitalization increased from 8.3 (2018) to 10.2 (2021) Figure 10.22. Suicide mortality in Orange County declined from 10.1 per 100,000 in 2018 to 9.1 in 2021. Despite this, it is still higher than the Mid-Hudson and state average Figure 10.23.

10.3 Preventative Screenings

Preventive screenings and chronic-condition monitoring show mixed performance across indicators. Colorectal cancer screening was 61.7% (2018) Figure 10.24, cervical cancer screening was 88.8% (2018) Figure 10.25, and mammography was 73.3% (2021) with prior values shown for comparison Figure 10.26.
