3  Data Sources and Indicator Selection

To create this document, the following data sources were utilized. In certain instances, data were obtained by special request and are not readily available to the public.

  • American Community Survey (ACS): Conducted by the US Census Bureau, the ACS is an ongoing nationwide survey that provides detailed demographic, social, economic, and housing data. The Orange County (OC) Department of Health (DOH) Division of Epidemiology and Public Health Planning utilizes 5-year estimates to ensure reliable data for smaller geographic areas and subpopulations. This resource is the primary source for monitoring social determinants of health, including poverty status, educational attainment, health insurance coverage, and housing cost burden. Secondary source

  • Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS): Coordinated by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), this annual telephone survey collects data on health-related risk behaviors, chronic conditions, and the use of preventive services. For local planning, OCDOH Division of Epidemiology and Public Health (PH) Planning utilizes the New York State (NYS) Expanded BRFSS, which provides county-level estimates through increased sampling to allow for statistically significant analysis of health disparities within OC. Secondary source

  • Community Asset Survey (CAS): Developed by the OCDOH Division of Epidemiology and PH Planning, the CAS is a tool from Mobilizing for Action through Planning and Partnership (MAPP) 2.0 Community Context Assessment. It captures residents’ perceptions of community assets, quality of life, and prominent health concerns through in-person engagement at local events and online via SurveyMonkey. This dataset includes 2022 and 2025 data, allowing for the identification of shifting community priorities and the existence of disparities in resource access. Primary source

  • Community Health Survey: Conducted by the Siena College Research Institute, this survey utilizes a dual-frame (landline and cell phone) random-digit-dial mode supplemented by an online panel. To ensure statistical representativeness, the sample includes oversamples of Latino, Black, and low-SES households, with data weighted to the most current American Community Survey 5-year estimates. The OCDOH Division of Epidemiology and PH Planning maintains data from 2018, 2022, 2023, and 2024 to facilitate longitudinal comparisons of community perceptions and health behaviors. Primary source

  • Community-Level Youth Development Evaluation Survey (CLYDE): This validated youth assessment satisfies Drug-Free Communities reporting requirements and is conducted biennially within participating OC school districts. Utilizing the research-based Communities That Care framework, CLYDE collects anonymous data on youth substance use prevalence, mental health, bullying, and specific developmental risk and protective factors across the family, school, and community domains. Primary source

  • Healthy People 2030: Guided by the US Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), this initiative provides 10-year national objectives for improving the health of all Americans. It features measurable goals with a heightened focus on health equity and social determinants of health, serving as the primary foundation and benchmarking framework for the 2025-2030 NYS Prevention Agenda. Secondary source

  • Institute for Traffic Safety Management and Research (ITSMR): An academic research center within the University at Albany’s Rockefeller College of Public Affairs and Policy, ITSMR manages the Traffic Safety Statistical Repository. This platform offers 10 years of finalized and preliminary data on police-reported crashes and traffic tickets across NYS. It enables OC to analyze trends in impaired driving, pedestrian safety, and occupant protection (seat belt use) at both the county and municipal levels. Secondary source

  • Mid-Hudson (M-H) Community Partner Survey: An online survey distributed by the Hudson Valley PH Collaborative to those who provide services in the region. Primary source

  • National Center for HIV, Viral Hepatitis, STD, and Tuberculosis Prevention AtlasPlus: This interactive CDC tool provides nearly 20 years of surveillance data on HIV, viral hepatitis, STDs, and tuberculosis. It integrates social determinants of health indicators, allowing OC to analyze disease trends alongside social and economic data at national, state, and county levels. Secondary source

  • New York State Cancer Registry: Mandated by PH Law Section 2401, this population-based registry collects comprehensive data on incidence, stage at diagnosis, and treatment for all reportable cancers (except certain skin cancers). It receives reports from hospitals, laboratories, and clinicians, ensuring high-quality surveillance used to monitor the burden of cancer and evaluate prevention programs. Secondary source

  • New York State Childhood Lead Poisoning Prevention Program (CLPPP): As the largest program of its kind in the US, CLPPP funds local health departments to identify and remediate lead hazards in high-risk housing. The program monitors blood lead levels through mandated provider reporting and utilizes a lowered actionable threshold of 5 micrograms per deciliter (ug/dL) to trigger public health interventions and environmental inspections. Secondary source

  • New York State Communicable Disease Annual Reports: Published by the NYSDOH Bureau of Communicable Disease Control, these documents provide annual case counts and rates per 100,000 for mandated reportable diseases. Data are available at the state and county levels, often including five-year historical trends and demographic breakdowns by age and sex. Secondary source

  • New York State Communicable Disease Electronic Surveillance System (CDESS): The reporting of suspected or confirmed communicable diseases is mandated under the NYS Sanitary Code (10NYCRR 2.10). While physicians have primary reporting responsibility, school nurses, laboratory directors, and healthcare facilities are also required to report. All reportable data from the Electronic Clinical Laboratory Reporting System (ECLRS) are integrated into CDESS for timely investigation. LHDs review these records to initiate, confirm, or dismiss cases based on standardized case definitions. Primary source

  • New York State Department of Health Bureau of Communicable Disease Control: This Bureau has broad oversight for the surveillance and control of more than 70 reportable and emerging infection diseases mandated under the NYS Sanitary Code. BCDC conducts epidemiological investigations, manages routine disease reporting, and provides technical assistance to local health departments for outbreak response and rabies post-exposure management. Secondary source

  • New York State Department of Health Bureau of Vital Records: This Bureau is responsible for the registration and preservation of vital events. It serves as the official repository for births, deaths, and fetal deaths occurring in NYS outside of NYC and maintains marriage and divorce records for the entire state. This Bureau is the primary authority for issuing certified copies of these records and providing the underlying data for statewide public health surveillance. Secondary source

  • New York State Department of Health Community Health Indicator Reports (CHIRS): This dashboard tracks approximately 350 indicators organized by 15 health topics, consolidating data from former County Health Assessment Indicators reports. It provides interactive visualizations, including tables, maps, and trend graphs, allowing OC to compare its performance against regional and statewide benchmarks. Secondary source

  • New York State Department of Health County Health Indicators by Race/Ethnicity (CHIRE): This map-based tool provides health indicator data disaggregated by race and ethnicity at the state and county levels. It covers mortality, chronic diseases, substance abuse, and social determinants of health, allowing for direct comparisons between OC’s minority health outcomes and the Rest of State averages by race and ethnicity to identify and monitor health disparities at the county level. Secondary source

  • New York State/Region Cancer Statistics Dashboards: These interactive dashboards provide streamlined access to comprehensive cancer data, including incidence, mortality, trends, stage at diagnosis, survival, and prevalence. Organized by state, regional, and county levels, they allow for site-specific analysis used to monitor the cancer burden and evaluate the impact of local screening initiatives. Secondary source

  • New York State Department of Health Office of Sexual Health and Epidemiology: This office is responsible for conducting Sexually Transmitted Infections (STI) surveillance related to screening, disease morbidity, and HIV/STI Partner Services intervention activities. OSHE oversees surveillance for chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis, and mpox for NYS excluding New York City (NYC) and provides technical assistance to local health departments to ensure data-driven disease control. Secondary source

  • New York State Education Department: This public portal provides comprehensive NYS school and district report cards containing data on student enrollment (by race, gender, and disability), graduation rates, and English Language Learner status. It also includes critical social determinants of health metrics, such as chronic absenteeism, free and reduced-price lunch eligibility, and school safety incidents. Secondary source

  • New York State HIV Surveillance System: Conducted by the AIDS Institute Bureau of HIV/AIDS Epidemiology, this system monitors the HIV epidemic through mandated state reporting. It utilizes ECLRS and clinician-based reporting to collect comprehensive data on diagnoses and disease monitoring. This system serves as the primary data source for the NYS HIV/AIDS Annual Surveillance Reports and tracks progress toward Ending the Epidemic goals. Secondary source

  • New York State Opioid Data Dashboard: This interactive visual platform tracks nearly 100 indicators at the state and county levels to monitor the overall burden of the overdose epidemic. It provides critical data on fatal and nonfatal overdoses (including opioids, heroin, and cocaine), controlled substance prescribing, and opioid use disorder treatment admissions. Secondary source

  • New York State Prescription Monitoring Program Dashboard: This database tracks the prescribing and dispensing of controlled substances to identify patterns of use and potential misuse. The data are the property of NYS and may be used solely for public health statistical analysis within a Local Health Department; any effort to identify a specific individual, provider, or entity is strictly prohibited. The OCDOH Division of Epidemiology and PH Planning maintains written approval to share the data contained within this Community Health Assessment. Secondary source

  • New York State Prescription Monitoring Program Registry: This secure database provides practitioners with a patient’s dispensed controlled substance history for the past five years, including data reported by pharmacies in NYS and participating interstate partners. Established under the I-STOP law, this registry allows providers to evaluate treatment history and identify potential misuse or “doctor shopping” before prescribing. Secondary source

  • New York State Prevention Agenda Tracking Dashboard: This is an interactive visual presentation of the most current tracking indicator data to track progress of the NYS’s Health Improvement Plan at state and county levels. It serves as a key source for monitoring progress that communities around the state have made regarding meeting the Prevention Agenda objectives. Secondary source

  • New York State Quality Indicators Dashboard: A comprehensive public health surveillance tool managed by NYSDOH. It provides accessible, county-level data across various health domains, such as chronic disease, maternal health, and environmental safety, to support community health assessment and planning. The dashboard allows users to compare local performance against NYS excluding NYC and the M-H Region benchmarks using standardized measures like age-adjusted rates. The data contained in the NYS Quality Indicators Dashboard are the property of the NYS and may be used solely for the purposes of public health statistical analysis by authorized users. Any effort to determine the identity of a specific individual, provider, or entity, or to use the information for any purpose other than for public health statistical analysis, is prohibited. The OCDOH Division of Epidemiology and PH Planning has written approval to share the data contained within this Community Health Assessment. Secondary source

  • New York State Sexually Transmitted Infections Data and Statistics Dashboard: This interactive web-based platform provides public access to surveillance data for reportable STIs across NYS. It empowers healthcare providers, policymakers, and the public with up-to-date data to inform community-level prevention and treatment strategies. The platform specifically supports a “syndemic approach,” addressing the intersection of STIs with HIV/AIDS, viral hepatitis, and substance use epidemics. Secondary source

  • New York State Student Weight Status Category Reporting System: A system that collects aggregated weight status data, including underweight, healthy weight, overweight, and obesity categories, from required school health certificates for children in public schools outside of NYC. These biennial reports provide essential district, county, and regional prevalence estimates that help health departments and schools identify trends, target resources, and evaluate the effectiveness of childhood obesity prevention interventions. Secondary source

  • New York Statewide Planning and Research Cooperative System (SPARCS): SPARCS is a comprehensive all-payer reporting system that captures patient-level characteristics, diagnoses, and charges for hospital inpatient stays and outpatient visits, including emergency department admissions and ambulatory surgery. To protect privacy, SPARCS has three access levels: Public Data for de-identified trends, Limited Research Data without direct identifiers, and Identifiable Data, which requires Data Protection Review Board approval. Secondary source

  • Orange County Department of Social Services: The OC Department of Social Services provides temporary financial and social service assistance to help eligible individuals and families lead safe and independent lives. Administered under NYS Social Services Law (Chapter 55 and Title 18 of the NYCRR), the department manages programs for Supplemental Nutrition Assistance (SNAP), Medicaid eligibility, Temporary Assistance, and Child Protective Services. This resource provides vital data for monitoring economic stability and food security within the county. Secondary source

  • Orange County Office of the Medical Examiner (OME): The OCOME investigates sudden, unexpected, and unnatural fatalities occurring within OC to ensure accurate cause and manner of death certification. This includes any death not due to 100% natural causes, workplace injuries, unattended deaths, homicides, suicides, and drug-related fatalities (overdoses). As the OCME investigates all jurisdictional deaths occurring within the county, data include non-residents but exclude residents who died elsewhere. Primary source

  • Small Area Income and Poverty Estimates Program (SAIPE): Conducted by the US Census Bureau, SAIPE provides the only single-year income and poverty statistics for all 3,143 US counties and 13,126 school districts. This program combines ACS data with administrative records to produce model-based estimates of median household income and childhood poverty. These data are a primary resource for OC to identify economic disparities and guide federal funding allocations, such as Title I school grants. Secondary source

  • STI Dashboard New York: This interactive web-based platform provides public access to surveillance data for reportable STIs across NYS. It features data visualizations for chlamydia, gonorrhea, and syphilis, allowing users to filter by sex, age group, and race/ethnicity at both the statewide and county levels. The dashboard is designed to support a “syndemic approach” to address the intersecting epidemics of HIV, STIs, viral hepatitis, and substance use. Secondary source

  • US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS): The BLS is the primary federal agency responsible for measuring labor market activity, working conditions, price changes, and productivity in the economy. As an independent statistical agency, it provides essential data to support public and private decision-making. Secondary source

  • US Census Bureau: The Census Bureau publishes population estimates and demographic components of change, such as births, deaths, and migration. These data can be sorted by characteristics such as age, sex, and race, as well as by national, state, and county location. The Census Bureau provides the official population estimates and demographic components of change, including births, deaths, and migration, necessary for calculating local health rates. Through the ACS and Population Estimates Program, data can be disaggregated by age, sex, race, ethnicity, and geography to identify socio-economic disparities. Secondary source

  • Vital Statistics of New York State: The Vital Statistics of NYS is a registry maintained by the NYS Bureau of Vital Records (BVR), part of the NYSDOH, covering births, deaths, and fetal deaths occurring in NYS outside of NYC, as well as statewide marriage and divorce records. Beyond record-keeping, it serves as a data source for annual statistical reports used in public health surveillance and policymaking. OCDOH Division of Epidemiology and PH Planning requested and analyzed data from Vital Records for inclusion in this CHA. Vital Records data do not include OC births or deaths recorded in NYC. All rates calculated from the Vital Records data use ACS 5-year population estimates, except for the age intervals <1 year and 1-9 years, which are based on crude live births in OC. The opinions, results, findings, and/or interpretations of the data contained within the CHA are the responsibility of the OCDOH Epidemiology and PH Planning staff and do not necessarily reflect the official policy or position of the NYSDOH or BVR.
