5  Orange County Department of Health

5.1 History

The Orange County Department of Health (OCDOH) was formed in 1969 as a full-service department in a chartered county with legislative oversight and an advisory Board of Health. The Department operates under the New York State Public Health Code and Titles 10 and 6 of the Official Compilation of Codes, Rules, and Regulations of the State of New York. Prior to 1969, public health services were provided through the NYSDOH district office in Middletown, which also served Sullivan and Putnam counties and the City of Beacon in Dutchess County. In the 1960s, NYSDOH encouraged the creation of county health departments in counties with a population base of 100,000 or more to better meet resident needs. NYSDOH and the public health offices in the county’s cities initially provided staff for the newly formed OCDOH.

5.2 Mission

In partnership with our community, OCDOH protects, promotes, and improves the health and quality of life of everyone in Orange County.

5.3 Scope of Services

The Department provides public health services countywide. Outreach, prevention, education, and intervention services are strategically located in communities of high need. OCDOH consists of nine main divisions: Administration, Community Health Outreach, Early Intervention Services, Emergency Preparedness, Environmental Health, Epidemiology and Public Health Planning, Health Equity, the Medical Examiner’s Office, and Public Health Nursing.

Overall direction and leadership for the Department are led by the Acting Commissioner of Health and Deputy Commissioners of Health. Jennifer Roman, DO, is the current Acting Commissioner of Health and the Medical Examiner. OCDOH has two Deputy Commissioners: Lisa Lahiff, JD, MHA, and Gretchen Riordan.

5.4 Division of Community Health Outreach (CHO)

The Division of Community Health Outreach (CHO) is led by director Jill Boyd and has offices in Newburgh, Middletown, and Port Jervis. CHO provides outreach, information, and referrals to individuals at high risk of contracting chronic or communicable diseases or requiring management of acute or chronic health conditions. CHO consists of many programs that provide resources and education to the communities they serve and target different public health issues to improve health and quality of life.

These programs include:

  • Healthy Neighborhoods and Childhood Lead Poisoning Prevention Programs, collectively known as Lead Safe Orange, monitor the results of mandatory childhood lead poisoning testing, provide case management for lead-poisoned children, and identify and address potential lead hazards. Lead Safe Orange targets areas in the cities of Newburgh, Middletown, and Port Jervis that are at high risk of lead hazards and meet residents directly in their homes to provide education and inspections to reduce lead exposure.
  • Lyme Disease and West Nile Virus Prevention Program focuses on reducing the incidence of Lyme and West Nile in Orange County by working with the public and healthcare providers to provide educational materials and tick removal kits. Staff are also able to assist in identifying ticks that potentially carry Lyme disease.
  • The Pregnant or Parenting People’s Initiative (funded through the Perinatal Infant Community Health Collaborative) operates in Monroe, Newburgh, New Windsor, Middletown, Port Jervis, Highland Falls, Chester, Goshen, Highland Mills, Pine Bush, Walden, and Town of Wallkill, and aims to improve maternal and infant health outcomes through individual-level needs assessments, case coordination, and referrals for high-need women and their infants. Community involvement, both by staff and the women they serve, is also prioritized to promote policy change and community-level health interventions.
  • Public Health Education works with hospitals, healthcare providers, schools, businesses, local coalitions and agencies, local media, and the public to provide education on current health topics and disseminate public health messages.
  • Adolescent Tobacco Use Prevention Act, JUUL Vaping Prevention, and Enforcement provide services to eliminate the use of tobacco and vapor products, particularly among the youth population, and prevent the long-term health consequences that can result from tobacco use.
  • Creating Healthy Schools and Communities is a five-year grant focused on increasing opportunities for physical activity and improved nutrition in high-need communities in New York State. In Orange County, the grant is administered in Middletown, Newburgh, Port Jervis, Highland Falls, Pine Bush, and Minisink. The program aims to implement food service guidelines with worksites and community settings; improve policies, practices, and environments for physical activity and nutrition in early care and education (ECE) settings and schools; and implement community planning and active transportation interventions in municipalities to increase safe and accessible physical activity.
  • Breastfeeding Friendly New York is a five-year grant focused on providing a comprehensive, community-wide approach to implement policy, systems, and environmental changes in support of breastfeeding, chest feeding, and lactation. The grant serves the cities of Middletown, Newburgh, and Port Jervis, and the towns of Deerpark, Highlands, Mount Hope, New Windsor, and Wallkill.

5.5 Division of Early Intervention Services (EIS)

The Division of Early Intervention Services (EIS) is led by director Sandra Brownsey, MS, CAS. The primary office is located in Goshen with a small satellite office in Newburgh. EIS oversees the provision of services to children with developmental delays, learning disabilities, or other special health care needs. The programs within the Division include Early Intervention and Child Find (ages 0-3), Preschool Services (ages 3-5), and Children and Youth with Special Health Care Needs (ages 0-21). All programs are voluntary and at no cost to families.

Early Intervention evaluates children to assess development and determine eligibility for services such as Speech Therapy, Occupational Therapy, Physical Therapy, and/or Special Instruction. Child Find screens and monitors the development of children by completing milestone checklists and providing parents with resources to enhance learning. Preschool Services are determined at the school district level by a child’s Committee on Preschool Special Education (CPSE). The Children and Youth with Special Health Care Needs program provides timely information and resources to families when a child has a developmental, behavioral, medical, or other special need. Local resources include parent support groups, adaptive recreation, sibling supports, mental health experts, food banks, and health care providers.

5.6 Public Health Emergency Response & Preparedness (PHEP)

Public Health Emergency Response & Preparedness (PHEP) is directed by Bryan Welsh and is based in the Orange County Emergency Services center. The division is responsible for planning and coordinating responses to public health emergencies in Orange County. PHEP works closely with the MRC and county, state, and regional partners, and serves as a liaison with the Department of Emergency Services to integrate public health into emergency operations. The division supports preparedness activities, emergency response, and recovery efforts, including mass distribution operations and vaccination Points of Dispensing. PHEP/MRC are also active in non-emergency health screening events, providing blood pressure and non-fasting cholesterol screenings and education.

5.7 Division of Environmental Health

The Division of Environmental Health is led by Principal Public Health Engineer Steven Gagnon, PE, MPH, who supervises the Bureau of Sanitary Engineering, and Principal Public Health Sanitarian Timothy Gaeta, who supervises the Bureau of Sanitary Control. Environmental Health is responsible for enforcing the New York State Sanitary Code to prevent and control environmental threats to public health. The Bureau of Sanitary Engineering directly monitors over 250 public water supply systems and conducts reviews of plans for new and modified public water supply systems; sewage disposal systems for realty subdivisions, food service, and temporary residence operations; water treatment systems for non-public water supply systems; and newly constructed swimming pools. Sanitary Engineering staff collects about 125 routine coliform samples per month to ensure community water system compliance, as well as additional samples collected during sanitary surveys of water systems and for further monitoring of positive samples.

The Bureau of Sanitary Control inspects over 1,500 food service operations, including restaurants, school lunch programs, mobile food establishments, temporary food service operations, and vending machines, to maintain proper sanitary conditions. Additionally, Sanitary Control inspects temporary residences, children’s camps, farm labor camps, public pools, bathing beaches, mobile home parks, recreational parks and campsites, public functions, and agricultural fairs for clean water, proper sewage disposal, and general safety and sanitation for residents visiting or living at these facilities. Sanitary Control staff is also responsible for following up on reports of public health nuisance complaints made by county residents, such as rodent or insect infestations and improper sewage disposal, enforcing the New York State Clean Indoor Air Act, processing specimens for rabies testing at NYSDOH’s Rabies Laboratory, and evaluating homes of children with high blood lead levels. Sanitary Control is also responsible for enforcement of the Orange County Sanitary Code, which deals with property maintenance issues brought to our attention through complaints. In addition to the above programs, Sanitary Control has a very robust Lead Based Paint program consisting of Childhood Lead Cases, Primary Prevention Lead Cases, and the newly created Lead Rental Registry.

5.8 Health Equity

Laurisa Sampson serves as the Director of Health Equity for the Orange County Department of Health and is based in the Newburgh office at 130 Broadway. The Health Equity Office works in partnership with residents, community organizations, and healthcare providers to advance health equity across Orange County. The office strives to strengthen community-driven, data-informed approaches that address social factors influencing health and support equitable access to preventive services, including recommended health screenings. Through inclusive collaboration and a focus on long-term impact, the Health Equity Office is committed to creating conditions where all residents have the opportunity to achieve good health—because in Orange County, health grows here for everyone.

5.9 Division of Epidemiology and Public Health Planning

The Division of Epidemiology and Public Health Planning is led by director Jacqueline Lawler, MPH, and has offices in Goshen and Newburgh. Epidemiology is responsible for assessing and monitoring the population health status and needs of the county, investigating and addressing health problems affecting residents, providing communication and education to various audiences with various levels of health literacy, and improving public health functions through ongoing evaluation and continuous quality improvement. Epidemiology staff collaborate with community stakeholders to provide education and coordinate efforts and resources for disease surveillance.

5.10 Medical Examiner’s (ME) Office

The Medical Examiner’s (ME) Office is led by Medical Examiner Jennifer Roman, DO, and has an office in Goshen. The ME Office investigates sudden, unexpected, and unnatural fatalities to ensure accurate cause and manner of death certification. The ME Office works with LiveOn NY to facilitate tissue retrieval from decedents for potentially lifesaving research and transplantation. They also assist the District Attorney’s Office to investigate cases and provide testimony for grand jury and trial and collaborate with the Hudson Valley Crime Analysis Center to confirm drug-related fatalities.

5.11 Division of Public Health Nursing

The Division of Public Health Nursing is led by director Heather Boss, RN, BSN, and has offices in Goshen, Middletown, and Newburgh. The division conducts investigations into communicable and vaccine-preventable diseases and serves residents aged two months and older through programs focused on communicable disease, immunization, sexually transmitted infections (STIs), and tuberculosis (TB). Public Health Nursing offers child immunization clinics for all recommended vaccines, as well as adult and travel immunization clinics. Child immunization clinics are available at no charge for eligible children under 18 years and for specific vaccines for high school students aged 19 years and older. Nursing staff also runs Latent TB testing clinics and conducts follow-up on suspected and confirmed TB cases, including contact investigations, administration of anti-TB medication, and regular monitoring of patients. The Public Health Nursing Sexual Health program offers free testing and treatment of STIs. A voluntary service is also offered to assist patients diagnosed with an STI in notifying partners who were potentially exposed to reduce the risk of transmission.
